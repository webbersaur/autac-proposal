What Is a Coiled Cord?

A coiled cord is an electrical cable wound into a helical spring shape that extends under tension and retracts to its compact resting length when released. The helical geometry is permanently heat-set into the jacket material during manufacturing, giving the cord elastic spring memory without any moving parts. A typical coiled cord extends to roughly five times its retracted length and returns automatically when the load is removed.

The product spans an enormous range of applications. The same coiled-cord geometry that carries milliamps on 26 AWG signal lines is also used for 600-volt, 30-amp portable power on 10 AWG conductors. What unifies them is the self-retracting behavior the helical construction produces — the cord stays out of the way when it isn't needed and reaches when it is.

How Coiled Cords Differ from Standard Cables

A regular cable has to be coiled and uncoiled by hand and stays where you leave it. A coiled cord retracts on its own, eliminating tangles and trip hazards at the workbench, on a vehicle dash, or alongside a piece of moving equipment. Beyond convenience, coiled cords also handle repeated flex cycles better than straight cable. The helical geometry distributes mechanical stress along the entire coil rather than concentrating it at fixed bend points, which is why coiled cords are specified for portable equipment, robotic arms, retracting tool stations, and medical devices that move thousands of times over their service life.

Construction: Conductors, Insulation, Jacket, Shield

Every Autac coiled cord is built up from four engineered layers:

  • Conductors — finely stranded copper sized from 26 AWG (signal) up to 10 AWG (heavy power), in counts from 2 through 25.
  • Insulation — PVC, polyurethane, or thermoset rubber chosen for the voltage rating and operating environment.
  • Jacket — PVC for general use or Auta-Prene, our proprietary thermoset compound, for superior coil memory, chemical resistance, and a low coefficient of friction that keeps the cord sliding through fixtures without snagging.
  • Optional shield — spiral-wrap or foil with drain wire, providing 90%+ EMI/RFI coverage for signal-integrity applications.

The cord is then heat-cured around a mandrel to set the helix geometry permanently. The result is a cable that retracts to a defined length and returns to that length cycle after cycle, for the life of the cord.

How to Specify a Coiled Cord

Most coiled cord inquiries stall in the same place: the application is clear but the cord is not described in terms a manufacturer can quote from. Six values cover almost every build. Send those and we can price a stock catalog number or a custom run without a round of questions first.

1. Retracted Length

The resting length of the coiled section, measured from the end of one lead to the start of the other. This is the number that determines how much cord is out of the way when the equipment is idle. Autac stocks retracted lengths of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 15, and 20 inches, and cuts custom lengths outside that range.

2. Extended Working Length

How far the cord actually has to reach in service. A heat-set coil extends to roughly five times its retracted length, so a 10 inch coil gives about 50 inches of working reach. Specify the reach you need rather than the coil you think you need, and we will work backwards to the retracted length. Sizing a coil to its absolute limit shortens its life; the cord should reach its working distance with tension left in it.

3. Conductor Count and Gauge

The stock catalog runs 2 to 4 conductors in 10 through 18 AWG, which covers most portable power and control applications. Custom builds run from 26 AWG signal conductors up to 10 AWG for heavy power, in counts to 25. Conductors are bare copper on power cords and tinned copper on communications, control, test lead, and shielded constructions.

4. Voltage and Current

Stock cords are rated 300V or 600V, carrying 5A to 30A depending on gauge and construction. UL types in the catalog include SVT, SVTO, SJT, SJTO, SO, SOW, and CMG. If you are working to a specific UL type, name it; that single value often settles the jacket, insulation, and voltage rating at once.

5. Jacket Material

TPE, TPR, or PVC, covered in detail below. If the cord will see oil, solvents, sustained cold, or heavy abrasion, say so here rather than leaving it to be discovered in the field.

6. Shielding and Terminations

Shielding is specified when the cord carries signal alongside anything electrically noisy. Autac stocks both braided and foil shielded constructions with a drain wire. Terminations are the last question: molded plug, bare leads for OEM assembly, or a connector you supply or name.

UL Listed or UL Recognized?

Both appear in the catalog and the difference matters at approval time. A UL Listed cord is certified as a finished product and can stand alone in the field. A UL Recognized cord is a component certified for building into a larger assembly that carries its own approval. Bare-end power cords, test leads, and the miniature constructions are Recognized; most finished power cords are Listed.

Jacket and Insulation Materials

The jacket compound does more than protect the conductors. It is the material the helix is heat-set into, so it governs how well the cord holds its coil over thousands of cycles, how it behaves in cold, and what it survives being dragged across. Autac builds coiled cords in three stock families plus its own thermoset compound.

TPE

A thermoplastic elastomer, and the default for flexible-duty coiled cords. TPE stays supple at low temperature, recovers its coil well, and takes repeated flexing without the jacket work-hardening. Autac stocks TPE in power cords, bare-end power cords, and shielded communications constructions, rated 300V.

TPR

A thermoplastic rubber, tougher than TPE and the choice where the cord is dragged, pinched, or exposed to oils. Autac stocks TPR power cords in 12 to 16 AWG, 3 conductor, at both 300V and 600V, carrying 13A to 20A. If a cord is failing on abrasion rather than on conductor fatigue, this is usually the move.

PVC

The general-purpose and most economical option, and the broadest part of the catalog. PVC covers stock power cords from 10 to 18 AWG at 300V and 600V up to 30A, communications and control cables, test leads, shielded cables, and the miniature constructions. PVC is the right answer for indoor equipment in a controlled environment; it stiffens in cold more than TPE does, so it is the wrong answer for outdoor or refrigerated service.

Auta-Prene

Autac's proprietary thermoset compound, developed for coil memory first. It holds its retracted length over a longer service life than a thermoplastic jacket, resists chemicals, and has a low coefficient of friction so the cord slides through fixtures and guides instead of snagging on them. It is specified where the cord is a wear part on equipment that has to keep running.

Choosing Between Them

In practice the decision follows the environment, not the electrical spec. Controlled indoor use goes to PVC. Cold, constant motion, or a cord that must recover its coil precisely goes to TPE. Oil, abrasion, and rough handling go to TPR. Long-service applications where the coil itself is the critical feature go to Auta-Prene. If you are unsure, describe where the cord lives and how often it moves, and we will specify the compound.
